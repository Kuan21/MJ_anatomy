BodyParts3D represents an adult male reference anatomy based on TARO MRI and anatomical illustration refinements. It is not a complete model of every possible human anatomical structure or variation. This interface is educational and is not a clinical tool.

## Female reproductive organs and serous membranes

The sex switch under Organs → Reproductive shows 33 female pelvic structures plus four pelvic bones (ovaries, uterine tubes, uterus and its ligaments, vagina, uterine arteries and veins). They come from Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein, Human Reference Atlas / HuBMAP, *3D Reference Organ Set for Female v1.5* (2023), CC BY 4.0, DOI https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM352.BTSQ.586. Only these 33 meshes are kept (`scripts/data/hra-female-pelvis.json.gz`, extracted once by `scripts/extract-hra-female.mjs`); they are scaled and moved onto the BodyParts3D pelvis by `scripts/prepare-female.mjs`. The pelvic bones are also from this set: in female mode the sacrum, coccyx and the compact bone of the ilium, ischium and pubis (`scripts/data/hra-female-pelvis-bones.json.gz`, extracted by `scripts/extract-hra-pelvis-bones.mjs`) replace the BodyParts3D hip bones and sacrum. They are registered onto the body by `scripts/fit-female-pelvis.mjs` (a similarity fit to the atlas pelvis, with the femoral head centres as landmarks; the same transform places the organs). The muscles, ligaments and perineum remain the BodyParts3D male model and sit within a few millimetres of the female bones; this is a known limitation.

Two things in the pelvis were moved by this project because their source course was wrong (`scripts/patch-atlas-vessels.mjs`, `scripts/lib/nerve-corrections.mjs`): the BodyParts3D superior gluteal veins (FJ3531, FJ3616) now leave the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen with the superior gluteal artery, and the Z-Anatomy superior gluteal nerves run through the foramen and between gluteus medius and minimus to tensor fasciae latae. Their shapes and thicknesses are the source meshes' own; only the centre lines were moved.

The breast (female only) uses the Human Reference Atlas *3D Reference Objects: Left / Right mammary gland, female, v1.1* (Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein, HuBMAP / Human Reference Atlas), CC BY 4.0: adipose tissue, mammary lobes, main lactiferous ducts and sinuses, nipple, areola with areolar tubercles, and suspensory ligaments. Sources: https://cdn.humanatlas.io/digital-objects/ref-organ/mammary-gland-female-left/v1.1/assets/3d-vh-f-mammary-gland-l.glb and https://cdn.humanatlas.io/digital-objects/ref-organ/mammary-gland-female-right/v1.1/assets/3d-vh-f-mammary-gland-r.glb (sha256 in `scripts/data/hra-female-breast.json.gz`). The meshes were thinned (`scripts/extract-hra-breast.mjs`) and then scaled to 55 % and bent onto the chest wall of the male BodyParts3D body by `scripts/paokao-structures/neck-breast-vulva.mjs`; the shape of the tissues is the source's own.

The pleura and pericardium layers are schematic shells written by this project (`scripts/prepare-serous.mjs`): each is an offset of the lung lobe or heart wall surface, not a measured structure. The five lung lobes are BodyParts3D 3.0 meshes (CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan, FMA7333, FMA7383, FMA7337, FMA7370, FMA7371).

## Historical assets (not included in the current release)

Earlier repository revisions included female reference anatomy: Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein, Human Reference Atlas / HuBMAP, *3D Reference Organ Set for Female v1.5* (2023). CC BY 4.0. Geometry adapted for this viewer.

- Source DOI: https://doi.org/10.48539/HBM352.BTSQ.586
- Dataset: https://lod.humanatlas.io/ref-organ/united-female/v1.5
- Original GLB: https://cdn.humanatlas.io/digital-objects/ref-organ/united-female/v1.5/assets/3d-vh-f-united.glb
- License: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

Adaptations: translated native meter/Y-up coordinates onto the stage, coincident vertices welded and source normals averaged, geometry simplified with a 0.2% per-structure relative error bound, and normals quantized. Colors and display systems are curated for this interface. All 888 source meshes are represented, with 1,073 source nodes available as selectable individual or compound concepts.

This is a reference assembly with whole-body surface and selected organs, including female reproductive anatomy. Its skeleton and muscle coverage is partial. It is not a complete model of every human structure or a single-person scan. Eight placenta/umbilical structures are classified under Pregnancy reference and hidden by default.

Separately licensed additions (BodyParts3D 3.0, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan, not the 4.0 CC BY terms above): the 47 facial/masticatory muscles (`models/facial/ATTRIBUTION.md`) and 28 structures that the 4.0 export omitted, such as latissimus dorsi and rectus abdominis (`models/supplement/ATTRIBUTION.md`).

## Schematic nerve and vein models (generated for this atlas)

Six structures of the suboccipital and posterior cervical region have no three-dimensional model in BodyParts3D or in the Z-Anatomy export (checked against [LluisV/Z-Anatomy](https://github.com/LluisV/Z-Anatomy) commit `6c7f9016bd5899ac8edafd31b9900c151df42ed6`, CC BY-SA 4.0: `NervousSystem100.fbx`, `CardioVascular41.fbx`, `MuscularSystem100.fbx` and the 1.0 export list contain only the grouping nodes "Cervical plexus" and "Cervical nerves", the "Occipital artery" and "Occipital vein", and no dorsal ramus, occipital, great auricular or suboccipital venous plexus mesh): suboccipital nerve (C1 dorsal ramus), greater occipital nerve (C2), third occipital nerve (C3), lesser occipital nerve, great auricular nerve and suboccipital venous plexus.

They are simplified tube models written by this project (`scripts/lib/nerve-schematics.mjs`): each tube follows the textbook course through landmarks measured on the BodyParts3D atlas meshes (atlas, axis, occipital bone, sternocleidomastoid, suboccipital muscles). They contain no third-party geometry. Calibre, curvature and branching are for orientation only, and every information card is marked 示意模型 (schematic model). The vertebral artery (BodyParts3D) lies 4.4–5.4 mm from the posterior arch of the atlas on both sides (`vertebralArteryReport`), i.e. beside C1 as expected.

## Brain detail mode: cut surfaces and CSF flow (generated for this atlas)

The brain detail mode (`#/organ/brain`) draws the Brain Project model (Z-Anatomy / BodyParts3D, CC BY-SA 4.0, see above) without changes. Two things in it are written by this project and contain no third-party geometry: (1) the solid cut surfaces of a section, built in the browser by rasterising the model's surfaces into a 1.2 mm voxel grid, filling each organ between its own surfaces and labelling tissue by distance to the cortical surface (the 2.4 mm grey-matter rim is schematic, not a measurement); (2) the CSF flow animation, whose ventricles, choroid plexus and superior sagittal sinus are the model's own meshes, while the foramina, apertures, cisterns, subarachnoid space, spinal cord, central canal, lumbar cistern, cauda equina and arachnoid granulations are simplified schematic shapes (the spinal part is shortened and not to scale). Both are marked as schematic in the interface.

## Vessel ends (connector and taper pieces)

The BodyParts3D 4.0 vessels are closed tubes cut off where the segmentation stopped. `scripts/prepare-vessel-ends.mjs` audits every vessel end (`docs/vessel-ends.md`) and adds, under the concept of the vessel they belong to, a thin curved connector tube where a vessel of the same kind lies within 12 mm, and a tapered tip where a small vessel ends in muscle, organ or fascia. These pieces are generated by this project from the vessel meshes themselves (no third-party geometry); they are schematic and their calibre is estimated from the mesh.

## Course-summary structures (paokao)

`scripts/prepare-paokao.mjs` builds, for structures named in the course summary that have no ready-made model in the sources, simplified models (the earlier statement here that the Z-Anatomy export contains none of them was wrong for the retinacula, tendon sheaths and aponeuroses, which the tendon batch below now takes from it): tubes for vessels and nerves, ribbons for ligaments and retinacula, thin shells for fasciae and aponeuroses, translucent boxes for spaces, and thin patches cut from the atlas bone and lung meshes for bone landmarks and lung surface marks. They are generated by this project from the textbook course of each structure (Gray’s Anatomy for Students, Moore’s Clinically Oriented Anatomy) on landmarks measured on the BodyParts3D atlas meshes; the patches are derived from BodyParts3D (CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan). The symmetrical sympathetic trunk and its ganglia now shown come from the Z-Anatomy `nervous.glb` already in the project (CC BY-SA 4.0). Every card says "Schematic model".

## Tendons, retinacula and tendon sheaths of the upper limb

- **Tendons (cut from the muscle meshes).** BodyParts3D draws the distal tendon of a muscle as the tapering end of the muscle mesh. `scripts/lib/tendon-cut.mjs` cuts each upper-limb muscle mesh at the musculotendinous junction (where the cross-section falls to 20 % of its largest value) into a muscle part and a tendon part that share the cut edge; the tendon part is subdivided and smoothed with that edge held fixed (`scripts/tendon/upper-limb-cuts.mjs`). The palmar aponeurosis is the part of the palmaris longus mesh distal to the flexor retinaculum. These parts are derived from BodyParts3D 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) and, for nothing here, from the 3.0 supplement. The muscle parts keep their ids and names; only their distal end is shorter.
- **Retinacula and tendon sheaths (Z-Anatomy).** The flexor and extensor retinaculum of the wrist, the six dorsal compartments, the common flexor tendon sheath and the sheaths of flexor pollicis longus, flexor carpi radialis and of the long head of biceps (intertubercular) are meshes of the Z-Anatomy muscular model, CC BY-SA 4.0 ([LluisV/Z-Anatomy](https://github.com/LluisV/Z-Anatomy), commit `6c7f9016bd5899ac8edafd31b9900c151df42ed6`, `MuscularSystem100.fbx` and `SkeletalSystem100.fbx`; its License.txt also asks for the BodyParts3D credit, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan). `scripts/extract-zanatomy-limb.mjs` converts centimetres to metres, registers the meshes to this atlas with one similarity transform per side and region (fitted on the bones both models share; residual 0.2 mm at the wrist, about 3 mm at the shoulder), simplifies them and vendors them in `scripts/data/zanatomy-upper-limb.json.gz`, so the build never downloads them. They are adapted and are offered under CC BY-SA 4.0. Only this repository (LluisV/Z-Anatomy) was used, not the nqwrc/3d-anatomy collection.
- **Shoulder-girdle ligaments (generated).** The coraco-acromial, costoclavicular, superior and inferior transverse scapular ligaments are flat bands with thickness written by this project between landmarks measured on the BodyParts3D bone meshes (`scripts/paokao-structures/ligaments-upper-limb.mjs`); they contain no third-party geometry and are marked as schematic on their cards.

## Tendons, retinacula and ligaments of the lower limb

- **Tendons (cut from the muscle meshes).** Same method as the upper limb (`scripts/lib/tendon-cut.mjs`, specs in `scripts/tendon/lower-limb-cuts.mjs`): quadriceps, hamstring, sartorius, gracilis, iliopsoas, popliteus, tibialis, fibularis, extensor and flexor tendons of the foot and plantaris are cut from BodyParts3D 4.0 muscle meshes (CC BY 4.0) into a muscle part and a tendon part sharing the cut edge. Where a source mesh ran through a bone (`clearBones`) the tendon is lifted out of it, and where it stopped a few millimetres short of its bone (`reachBone`) the end is moved onto the bone. The patellar ligament is the part of the rectus femoris mesh below the apex of the patella.
- **Retinacula and fasciae (Z-Anatomy).** The superior and inferior extensor retinacula of the ankle, the plantar aponeurosis and the medial and lateral patellar retinacula are meshes of the Z-Anatomy muscular model, CC BY-SA 4.0 ([LluisV/Z-Anatomy](https://github.com/LluisV/Z-Anatomy), commit `6c7f9016bd5899ac8edafd31b9900c151df42ed6`; its License.txt also asks for the BodyParts3D credit, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan). `scripts/extract-zanatomy-leg.mjs` converts centimetres to metres, registers them to this atlas with a similarity transform per side and region (bone centres, then iterated closest point on the bone surfaces; residual about 4 mm), simplifies them and vendors them in `scripts/data/zanatomy-lower-limb.json.gz`. Derived meshes are provided under CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Ligaments and retinacula built by this project.** The anterior and posterior talofibular, calcaneofibular, anterior tibiotalar and plantar calcaneonavicular (spring) ligaments, the fibular collateral ligament, the tendinous arch of soleus, the pes anserinus and the flexor and fibular retinacula of the ankle are bands, cords and sheets written by this project between landmarks measured on the BodyParts3D bone meshes (`scripts/paokao-structures/ligaments-lower-limb.mjs`); they contain no third-party geometry and are marked as schematic on their cards.
